Every building starts below ground. What goes wrong down there stays a problem for the life of the building: a basement that draws water cannot be sealed afterwards, and foundations that settle show up above as cracks. We carry out earthworks, foundations and drainage for residential, commercial and public buildings – on their own or together with the structural shell.

## What groundworks cover

Site clearance and excavation, the pit with shoring and slope stabilisation, dewatering, soil replacement and compaction. Then the foundations: blinding layer, strip and pad footings or a continuous ground slab, and on difficult ground, in agreement with the geotechnical engineer, deep foundations. Added to that the drainage – below-slab runs, chambers, land drains and the connection to the public sewer – and finally access roads, parking areas and sub-bases.

## The interface that slips most often

In practice the biggest programme risk on a project sits between earthworks and shell. The earthworks contractor hands over a formation, the shell contractor finds it other than planned – and while the two establish who owns the level difference, the crane stands idle. Where we do both, that discussion does not happen: one site manager, one survey, one warranty. At City Gate Nord in Hanover and for PVU-Group in Isernhagen the commission therefore covered groundworks, shell and fit-out together.

## The ground decides, not a rate per square metre

Between a shallow foundation on competent ground and an excavation with shoring and dewatering in groundwater lie worlds, for the identical building. That is why our costing starts with the soil report rather than with an experience figure. Where assumptions are necessary – about the soil class in areas without a borehole, for instance – we write them into the offer instead of pricing them in silently.

## It only gets watertight once

Basements below the design water level are built in watertight concrete. Dense concrete alone is not enough: what matters is the joint layout planned in advance, waterbars in the right places, reinforcement sized to limit crack widths, and curing that does not let the concrete dry out too early. Services, waterproofing and drainage are surveyed and documented before backfilling – after that nobody can reach them.
