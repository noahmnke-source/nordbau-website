  - q: How does your turnkey service differ from a management contractor?
    a: We build the loadbearing part ourselves. That shortens the chain between you and the site.
  - q: Can we award individual trades ourselves?
    a: Yes. Many clients keep building services or fit-out in their own hands. Our work then ends at the agreed point – usually the shell with a weathertight envelope. What is included and what is not is set out in the specification before contract.
  - q: How reliable is the handover date?
    a: The date is part of the contract, not a statement of intent. Behind it sits a programme with lead times for materials and specialist trades. In practice, slippage almost always comes from later changes or consents – both of which we discuss before they touch the date.
  - q: How is payment handled?
    a: Under a construction contract in accordance with German law (BGB), with instalments for work performed rather than by calendar. The fixed price applies to the agreed scope; variations arise only from changes you commission, and are priced in advance.

Turnkey construction, for us, does not mean administering a project. It means building the loadbearing part ourselves and adding the remaining trades so that a finished building is handed over – with one contract, one contact and one date.

## Why the shell is the precondition

A company that only brokers cannot vouch for dimensional accuracy. We can, because foundations, structure and floors come from our own hands. Every tolerance that arises in the shell is ours to answer for – and is not passed on as a problem for the fit-out.

## The interface that does not exist

Between shell and fit-out lies the most expensive joint on any site: two contracts, two sets of records, two views on who caused the deviation. Where both are the same commission, that argument never starts. For clients this is less a question of price than of peace of mind.

## What you get

A programme that puts every trade in sequence. Instalments for work performed. Measured records and test protocols at handover. And a site manager who still answers the phone afterwards.
