  - q: Is a bungalow more expensive than a two-storey house?
    a: Per square metre usually somewhat, because the slab and roof area are larger for the same living space. Against that stand the absence of a staircase and stairwell, a simpler build sequence and lower maintenance costs. Over the life of the building the difference narrows considerably.
  - q: Is a bungalow automatically accessible?
    a: Being on one level is not the same as being accessible. We plan step-free and with generous door widths as standard. The bungalow is the most consistent answer to a simple question: why climb stairs if everything can sit on one level? Living, sleeping, bathroom and kitchen open off short, step-free routes – a house that adapts to people rather than the other way round.

## One level, thoroughly considered

A good bungalow lives from its zoning. We separate the public area – kitchen-diner and living room facing the garden – clearly from the private wing with bedrooms and bathroom. A central hall holds both together without wasting area. Large window formats bring the site into the house: with only one level every room sits at garden level, with direct access to the terrace.

## Why timber frame suits a bungalow particularly well

A bungalow has a lot of roof and external wall relative to its living space – two areas where insulation decides running costs. This is exactly where timber frame construction shows its strength: highly insulated, slim components, made in the plant and sealed airtight. Slim walls also mean more internal floor area for the same external footprint.

## Built today, still right tomorrow

A bungalow is a decision for decades. We plan it so that it moves with its occupants: step-free transitions, generous door widths, a bathroom that can be adapted without touching the structure. What is comfort today is later the condition for staying in your own home.
