faq:
  - q: How does a modular house differ from a classic prefab house?
    a: In timber frame construction, flat wall, floor and roof elements are delivered and joined into a house on site; the fit-out follows on site. With a modular house, three-dimensional room cells are built that leave the plant almost complete – including bathroom, floors and finishes. On site they are only set and connected.
  - q: Can you tell the modules apart?
    a: From outside, generally not. Façade, roof edge and window formats run across the module joints. Inside, a partition at a module boundary can be slightly thicker; where a continuous room is wanted, we place the joint into a wall or a cupboard.
  - q: Can a module really be added later?
    a: If it is planned from the start, yes. We size the structure, foundations and the heating, water and electrical connections so a further module can be docked on. Without that preparation an extension becomes expensive – which is why we ask about it at the first meeting, even if it is not yet relevant.
  - q: Does the site need special access?
    a: Yes, and it is the most important point. Modules are delivered whole by low-loader and set with a mobile crane. Access, turning radii, overhead lines and the crane standing area are checked before contract. Where that is not possible, timber frame construction with flat elements is the better route.

The modular house takes prefabrication to its logical conclusion: instead of flat components, complete room modules are built in the plant – with floors, bathrooms, services and finishes. On site they are assembled like precisely made building blocks. The result is a house that stays more predictable and grows with your requirements.

## The hall delivers the finished house

The more steps happen under controlled conditions, the more precise and weather-independent the result. With a modular house the unit leaves the hall almost complete – checked, insulated, fitted out. On the plot we connect the modules; work on site is limited to the connections. For clients that means the shortest route from shell to move-in at maximum manufacturing quality.

## Planned to be extended, not just built

A modular house does not have to do everything from day one. Two modules now, a third later – we plan the structural and technical connections so later additions are possible without demolition and without compromise. That suits households whose space requirements will foreseeably change, as well as schemes made up of several identical units.

## The same substance as any other house

Modular construction does not mean temporary. Structure, insulation, building services and quality control match what we build into every detached house – including the Efficiency House 40 standard. The difference lies in the manufacturing process, not in the substance.
